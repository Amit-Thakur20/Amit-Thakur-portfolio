Case Study 03

Foodxp: an app that learns your taste and ends the daily “what should I cook?”

Here’s a look into the swipe, scoring, and AI-planning modules I built for the Foodxp B2C app.

0→1 Consumer App
Swipe-First Discovery
Recommendation & Learning Loop
AI Meal Planning

The problem

Every day, three times a day, the same question returns: what should I cook? Breakfast, lunch, dinner — over a thousand meal decisions a year, and most recipe apps make the decision harder, not easier.

Search-first apps assume you already know what you want. They hand you an endless list and leave the choosing to you — then forget everything the moment you close the app.

The real problem Users don’t need more recipes — they need the app to decide, so they can just cook.
No memory Recommendations never improve, so the app feels the same on day 100 as it did on day one.

How we knew we solved it

The bar: the app should decide better every single day, and never feel like a search box. Four signals tracked it.

  • Taste Score hit rate (Primary): The share of recommended recipes a user accepts — the model’s job is to climb it.
  • Learning velocity: How fast the taste map moves. The profile showed it plainly — 89 swipes already reshaping Tangy, Savoury, Creamy, and Herby.
  • Daily habit (streak + cooked): Proof the app answered the question well enough to bring the user back.
  • Decision time: Open to meal chosen — the number the whole product exists to shrink.

What I did

01

Reframed the problem

Renamed it from recipe discovery to meal decision-making, three times a day. The product decides first; the user confirms.

02

Designed the onboarding intake

A one-time capture — diet, cuisines, and time to cook — that seeds the recommendations before the first swipe.

03

Designed the learning loop

Every pass, like, skip, and cook feeds a per-user taste model, so the app sharpens with use.

04

Defined the Taste Score

One number on every recipe — the user-facing face of the model’s confidence, so recommendations never feel like a black box.

05

Built it end to end

Product logic, feature design, and UI/UX — designed and shipped without a handoff.

What I shipped

1. The Core Loop — Swipe, Like, Learn

The deck: Home greets the user with the day and a swipe-first deck — “Five fresh picks for your Friday.”

The card: each one carries a Taste Score badge (83% your taste), a meal tag (LUNCH), and the recipe’s time and calories.

The signals: cards layer as the user swipes, and PASS, LIKE, and SKIP each mean something — reject, reinforce, or defer.

2. Taste Score on Every Recipe

Every recipe — on Home, Explore, or a Plan — wears its match percentage (83%, 88%, 86%, 85%). The model shows its work, so the user sees the fit before they choose.

3. AI Meal Plan — “Your week, planned”

For each slot the AI suggests two options and the user picks one (pick one ✨), each with its own match — “A 79% match for your taste profile.”

User-added meals are marked “PLANNED BY YOU”; the rest are AI picks. The Nutrition card totals the day — “Today’s plan adds up to 2,364 / 2,500 kcal” — with protein, fat, and carbs tracked.

4. Explore — a feed that’s different for every user

An AI Collection article of the day, personalized to the user (“Exploring the Rich Flavors of Indian Cuisine” → Read the story), then taste-scored rails like Quick and Easy and Browse cuisines.

5. Profile — Your Food Personality

A computed Food Style — “Balanced Mindful Eater” — with a plain-English read of what it means, and the habit in three numbers: streak, cooked, and likes taught (1 day, 13 cooked, 89 likes taught).

Underneath, “Your taste map — learning from 89 swipes” shows Tangy, Savoury, Creamy, and Herby as live scores, above the Preferences carried in from onboarding — including Under 45 min.

Impact

  • Turned “what should I cook?” from a search into a recommendation — the app decides first, the user confirms.
  • Every interaction sharpens the next suggestion — one onboarding set grew into a live four-axis taste map in 89 swipes.
  • Removed the three-times-a-day decision burden — and balanced it, with plans that also track calories and macros.
  • Shipped end to end — product logic through UI/UX, no handoff.
